NB00: Project Overview

This document orients you to the Forschungsseminar project. It explains what you are building, why the problem is physically interesting, what equations govern it, and how to approach the work. Read it carefully — it is your reference throughout the semester. Everything else you need, you will find through independent study of the literature provided.

1. The Physics Problem

Guitar strings are not ideal: they are made of metal wire that resists bending as well as stretching. This bending stiffness causes different vibrational frequencies to travel at different speeds along the string — a phenomenon called dispersion. Dispersion makes the overtones of a real guitar string slightly sharp relative to the ideal harmonic series, producing the characteristic bright, slightly inharmonic timbre of the instrument. Capturing this effect accurately in a physical simulation is both musically important and numerically demanding.

Three fundamentally different approaches can be used to simulate this system: an analytical modal solution, a finite-difference time-domain (FDTD) simulation, and a Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN). Each has different strengths and weaknesses in terms of accuracy, computational cost, and flexibility. Your task is to understand, implement, and critically compare all three.

Governing Equation

The transverse displacement $u(x, t)$ of the string at position $x$ and time $t$ is governed by the following PDE, following the model of Luan et al. [1]:

$$\begin{aligned} \rho A \,\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} &- T \,\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + EI \,\frac{\partial^4 u}{\partial x^4} \\[6pt] &+ 2\rho A \sigma_0 \,\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} - 2\rho A \sigma_1 \,\frac{\partial^3 u}{\partial t\,\partial x^2} = 0 \end{aligned}$$

Each group of terms plays a distinct physical role:

  • The first two terms ($\rho A\,\partial_{tt} u$ and $-T\,\partial_{xx} u$) form the ideal wave equation — the standard model for a perfectly flexible string under tension.
  • The third term ($EI\,\partial_{xxxx} u$) is the bending stiffness term: it involves a fourth-order spatial derivative and is responsible for dispersion.
  • The last two terms model damping: $\sigma_0$ is a frequency-independent loss (viscous drag), and $\sigma_1$ introduces frequency-dependent loss (internal material friction).

Initial Conditions: the Pluck

The string is excited by a pluck: it is displaced into a triangular profile and released from rest. The apex of the triangle is at position $x_1$ with maximum displacement $y$:

$$u(x,\,0) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{y}{x_1}\,x, & 0 \le x \le x_1 \\[10pt] \dfrac{y}{L - x_1}\,(L - x), & x_1 < x \le L \end{cases} \qquad \frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(x,\,0) = 0$$

The condition $\partial_t u = 0$ at $t = 0$ means the string starts from rest — displaced but not yet moving.

Boundary Conditions

Both ends of the string are simply supported (pinned): displacement is fixed to zero and there is no bending moment at either end:

$$u(0,\,t) = u(L,\,t) = 0, \qquad \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}(0,\,t) = \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}(L,\,t) = 0$$

The first pair fixes the string's endpoints; the second pair enforces zero curvature at the supports, which is the mathematical expression of zero bending moment.

2. Your Approach

Phase 1 — Study and Literature Review

Before writing a single line of code, you must build a solid understanding of the methods and the problem domain. Work through the following topics independently, using the literature listed in Section 4 as your starting point. This understanding is a prerequisite for the topic understanding presentation.

  1. Read the main reference paper. Luan et al. [1] is your primary technical reference. Understand their problem formulation, PINN architecture, loss function, and results before anything else.
  2. Study PDEs and analytical solutions. Understand what a partial differential equation is, what boundary and initial conditions mean, and how to find an analytical modal solution through separation of variables. You will need to derive the modal solution for the stiff string yourself.
  3. Study FDTD simulation. Understand the finite-difference time-domain method: how continuous derivatives are approximated on a discrete grid, how the update equation is derived, and crucially, what determines numerical stability. Bilbao [3] and Välimäki et al. [4] are the key references here.
  4. Study Physics-Informed Neural Networks. Understand the PINN framework: how the PDE, initial conditions, and boundary conditions are incorporated into a neural network loss function. Raissi et al. [2] is the foundational reference; Luan et al. [1] shows the application to your specific problem.
  5. Study evaluation metrics. Understand how to compare numerical solutions quantitatively: L² error, L∞ error, relative error, and computational cost. You will need to justify your choice of metrics in the project report.
  6. Conduct a literature review on the state of the art of PINNs for guitar and musical instrument modelling, with a strong emphasis on the latest publications. Focus your search first on guitar strings; if the literature is sparse, broaden to string instruments, and then to musical instruments in general. Go beyond the references provided here — search for related work and critically assess what has been done, what works, and what remains open. Currency of sources matters: prioritise recent work over older surveys.

Literature review scope: Your review must cover three areas: (1) physical modelling of stiff strings, (2) foundational PINN methods, and (3) state-of-the-art and latest publications on PINN applications in musical acoustics. Prioritise work on guitar strings; if the literature is sparse, broaden to string instruments, then to musical instruments more generally. The references in Section 4 are your starting point — not your ending point. Use them to trace forward and backward through the literature, with a strong emphasis on the most recent work.

Phase 2 — Bottom-Up Implementation

Once you understand the methods, implement them using a bottom-up strategy: start with the simplest possible version of the PDE and progressively add complexity. At each level, implement all three methods (analytical, FDTD, PINN), evaluate them, and confirm they agree before moving to the next step. This approach catches bugs early, builds physical intuition, and produces a natural structure for your project report.

Step 1

Ideal Wave Equation — tension only

Strip away all complexity and start with the simplest string model, with no stiffness and no damping:

$$\rho A \,\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} - T \,\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} = 0$$

Derive the analytical modal solution, implement the FDTD scheme, and implement a PINN for this equation. Use the same initial and boundary conditions throughout (triangular pluck, simply supported ends). Evaluate all three and confirm they match before proceeding.

Step 2

Stiff String — add bending stiffness

Add the fourth-order bending stiffness term:

$$\rho A \,\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} - T \,\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + EI \,\frac{\partial^4 u}{\partial x^4} = 0$$

This is where dispersion appears. The analytical modal frequencies change, the FDTD stability condition becomes significantly tighter (the fourth-order term imposes a much stricter constraint on the time step), and the PINN must now compute fourth-order spatial derivatives through automatic differentiation. Extend each of your three implementations, evaluate, and compare again.

Step 3

Full Model — add damping

Finally, add the two damping terms to arrive at the complete model from Luan et al. [1]:

$$\begin{aligned} \rho A \,\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} &- T \,\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + EI \,\frac{\partial^4 u}{\partial x^4} \\[6pt] &+ 2\rho A \sigma_0 \,\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} - 2\rho A \sigma_1 \,\frac{\partial^3 u}{\partial t\,\partial x^2} = 0 \end{aligned}$$

With damping, the analytical solution gains exponential decay envelopes, the FDTD scheme must handle the mixed time–space derivative term, and the PINN loss must include the full residual. Evaluate all three methods on this final model and produce your quantitative comparison.

Figure 1: Bottom-up implementation strategy. At each complexity level, all three methods are implemented, evaluated, and validated before adding the next layer of physics.

Why bottom-up? Debugging a full stiff string PINN from scratch is extremely difficult. If you start at Step 1 and confirm each method works correctly on the ideal wave equation before adding stiffness, you will immediately know which term introduces any new behaviour or bug. Each step adds exactly one new mathematical feature — dispersion at Step 2, damping at Step 3 — so the differences between steps are physically interpretable and easy to reason about.

3. Your Tasks

The following tasks define the full scope of the project. They follow directly from the approach described in Section 2. All tasks must be documented in the scientific project report and covered in the final presentation.

  1. Literature review. Conduct a thorough literature review focusing on state-of-the-art and latest publications on PINNs applied to guitar string and musical instrument modelling in musical acoustics, covering physical modelling of stiff strings, foundational PINN methods, and recent PINN applications. Prioritise work on guitar strings; if literature is insufficient, broaden the search to string instruments, then to musical instruments in general. The starting references are listed in Section 4.
  2. Analytical modal solution. Following the bottom-up strategy, derive and implement the analytical modal solution at each level of PDE complexity (ideal wave equation → stiff string without damping → full stiff string model). Use this solution as the reference ground truth for all quantitative comparisons. No experimental or measured data is used — the analytical solution serves as the sole reference.
  3. FDTD simulation. Derive and implement the FDTD scheme at each complexity level. For each step, derive the stability condition and validate numerically against the analytical modal solution.
  4. PINN implementation. Implement a PINN at each complexity level, following the architecture and loss formulation of Luan et al. [1]. The PDE residual, the initial conditions, and all four boundary conditions must be incorporated into the loss function at every step. All comparisons are made against the analytical modal solution and the FDTD simulation — no experimental data is involved.
  5. Quantitative comparison. At each complexity level, and especially on the full model, select appropriate error metrics and compare all three methods quantitatively. Discuss accuracy, computational cost, numerical stability, and suitability for sound synthesis applications.
